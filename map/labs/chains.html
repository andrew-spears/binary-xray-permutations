Pick an arrangement. The board shows a solution with as few chains as possible. A chain is a sequence of stones in which each stone is higher up and further right than the one before. So far three chains have always been enough.

Reading the picture. Each stone is labelled with its diagonal (column − row). Stones of one colour form one chain, joined by a line. Two stones in different chains may still be one above the other; what matters is that no four stones are pairwise unrelated. Stones of a chain often sit in a row along an anti-diagonal: such a group uses neighbouring rows, neighbouring columns and every second diagonal.
