Pick an arrangement. Each bar is one stone, from the lowest diagonal up: its height says how many marks the stone sits above or below its evenly spaced place. A stone two or more above is high, two or more below is low. Dashed lines are tight cuts, where the arrangement splits into independent pieces.

What is proved. A piece has a proof, for every size and every slack, if no high stone comes before a low stone; or if it rises and then falls with at most 2 above and 4 below (or 4 above and 2 below); or if it is a gentle beginning (nothing high), then one hill with letters between −2 and 2, then a gentle end (nothing low). The board shows one solution; for the pieces without a proof it comes from a search, and all arrangements this small were checked by computer.

What is left. A high stone before a low stone. The proof for the first family walks along the word with a set of hole patterns that depends only on the slack; a high stone breaks that set, and how to carry it across the stretch from the first high stone to the last low one (shaded) is the open question.
